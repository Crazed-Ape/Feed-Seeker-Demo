# {{pick.game}} · Rulebook

*Fifth draft · 12 pages · for the playtest print*

## Page 1 · Welcome

You and your neighbors have one day to get the harbor ready for the festival. Finish every task before the tide runs out, and you all win together.

For 2 to 5 players, about 40 minutes.

## Page 2 · The harbor

The board shows the harbor and its six docks, joined by a path that runs all the way around the water:

1. The Pier
2. The Fish Market
3. Bakery Row
4. The Lighthouse
5. The Boathouse
6. The Ferry Slip

The path is a loop: after the Ferry Slip comes the Pier again. Each dock has one task space, where a festival task card sits during the game. Neighbors walk from dock to dock, do the work each task needs, and keep an eye on the water.

## Page 3 · Setup

1. Unfold the harbor board in the middle of the table.
2. Shuffle the 10 festival task cards. Place one face up on the task space of each dock, and return the other four to the box without looking at them.
3. Each player chooses a neighbor card and takes the matching pawn. Place every pawn on the Pier. Put the shells in a pile beside the board: this is the shell supply.
4. Shuffle the Tide deck. Deal four cards to each player in a game of two or three, or three cards each in a game of four or five.
5. Reveal five Tide cards face up in a row beside the board. This is the tide line. Ignore any waves on these first five cards: no task has shells on it yet, so there's nothing to wash away.
6. The player who most recently stood on a beach goes first.

Keep your Tide cards in your hand. You may show them to the others whenever you like. Give each player a reference card if you like: the deck has one.

## Page 5 · The cards

### Festival task cards

Each task card shows a name and five spaces, and each space shows a symbol: net, lantern, bread, flower or rope. A task is finished when all five of its spaces hold a shell.

### Tide cards

The Tide deck has 54 cards. Each card shows one symbol. Some also show a wave and a picture of a dock: those are the cards that wash work away at the end of a round (see page 8). One card in the deck is the ferry card.

### Neighbor cards

Each neighbor has one ability:

1. **The Herbalist:** once per turn, you may play a flower card as any symbol.
2. **The Baker:** when you take a card from the tide line, take two, then put one back.
3. **The Lamplighter:** you may move up to three docks instead of two.
4. **The Net Mender:** waves never wash shells off the task at your dock.
5. **The Harbor Master:** you may trade with a neighbor at any dock, not only your own.

### Card diagram

*Diagram: a festival task card and a Tide card, side by side, with the icon legend from the card layouts.*

1. **Symbol:** the kind of work this card does.
2. **Wave:** at the end of a round, this card washes one shell off the task at the dock shown.
3. **Dock picture:** where the wave lands.
4. **Task space:** place a shell here when you play a matching card.

## Page 6 · A turn

Play goes clockwise. On your turn, do these four steps in order:

1. Move
2. Take or trade
3. Work
4. Rest

The reference card has these four steps on the front and a short example on the back.

You may talk about your plans at any time, including on other players' turns. Talking is not a step. It's the whole game.

### 1. Move

Move your pawn up to two docks along the path, in either direction. You may also stay where you are. Any number of pawns can share a dock.

### 2. Take or trade

Choose one:

1. **Take:** take one card from the tide line into your hand. Don't refill the tide line.
2. **Trade:** swap one card with a neighbor at your dock. You both have to agree, and you can each look at what's on offer first.

## Page 7 · A turn, continued

### 3. Work

Play any number of Tide cards whose symbols match empty spaces on the task at your dock. For each card you play, take one shell from the supply and place it on the matching space. Played cards go to a discard pile beside the deck.

When the fifth shell lands on a task, the task is finished. Turn the card sideways so everyone can see it's done. Its shells stay where they are, and the tide can't touch them.

### 4. Rest

If you have more than five cards in your hand, discard down to five. Then the player on your left takes a turn.

### The ferry card

Instead of moving in step 1, you may play the ferry card to move your pawn to any dock. Then discard it.

## Page 8 · The Tide

The Tide deck is the clock. Nobody draws from it during a turn. It only shrinks at the end of each round, when the tide comes in.

A round ends after every player has taken one turn. Then:

1. Discard any cards left in the tide line.
2. Reveal five Tide cards from the deck to make a new tide line.
3. For each new card with a wave, find the dock it shows. If the task at that dock is unfinished and has at least one shell on it, remove one shell and return it to the supply.
4. The player on the left of this round's first player starts the next round.

## Page 9 · The Tide, continued

### Reading the water

1. Count the deck often. Five cards leave it every round, and when it can't give you five more, the day is over.
2. A task with four shells and a wave on the way is a task to finish this turn.
3. Finished tasks are safe. The tide only takes from work that isn't done.
4. The tide line is shared. Before you take a card, ask whether someone else needs it more.

### Waves and the Net Mender

If a wave lands on the dock where the Net Mender's pawn stands, nothing washes away. Plan the Net Mender's walk around the tasks you can't finish yet.

## Page 10 · The end of the game

The game ends at the end of the round in which you can't reveal five Tide cards. If every festival task is complete, you all win. If not, you all lose together, and probably play again.

Then turn the page and see how close you came.

## Page 11 · Scoring

Win or lose, it's worth knowing how close you came. Count the shells on the board. Every finished task holds five shells, so a harbor with all six tasks done scores 30. Shells the tide washed away don't count.

| Score | The festival |
|---|---|
| 30 | The lanterns are lit and the music starts on time. |
| 25 to 29 | The festival opens late, but it opens. |
| 20 to 24 | Half the town ends up helping. Try again tomorrow. |
| Under 20 | The tide had other plans. |
