# German for the Fair: Vocabulary

Version 1 · Lesson 1

## How to read the hints

The hints are sound-alikes, not exact spellings. Capital letters show where the weight goes. Say them the way they look in plain English and you'll be close.

## Lesson 1: hello, please, thank you

| # | German | Sounds like | Meaning | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guten Morgen | GOO-ten MOR-gen | Good morning | Until about eleven |
| 2 | Guten Tag | GOO-ten TAHK | Hello | The safe greeting for the rest of the day |
| 3 | Hallo | HAH-lo | Hi | Friendly, and fine with other exhibitors |
| 4 | Danke / Danke schön | DAHN-kuh / DAHN-kuh shern | Thanks / Thank you very much | Any time someone helps |
| 5 | Bitte | BIT-tuh | Please, and also "you're welcome" | Both directions |
| 6 | Entschuldigung | ent-SHOOL-di-gung | Excuse me, or sorry | Getting past someone, or getting attention |
| 7 | Auf Wiedersehen / Tschüss | owf VEE-der-zay-en / choos | Goodbye, formal / casual | Leaving a stand / leaving friends |

## Sound notes

1. The **ö** in **schön** is close to "er" said with rounded lips. "Shern" gets you most of the way.
2. The **ü** in **Tschüss** is an "ee" said with rounded lips. "Choos" is fine for now.
3. **Bitte** and **Danke** both end in a soft "uh", not an "ee" or an "ay".
4. **Entschuldigung** is long, but it breaks into four beats. Lean on the second one.

## Sie and du

German has two words for "you".

1. **Sie** (zee) is polite: strangers, suppliers, anyone selling you something.
2. **du** (doo) is for friends.

People at game fairs tend to move to **du** quickly. Start with **Sie** and follow the other person's lead. Nobody minds being treated politely.

## Practice

Say three of these out loud, anywhere, to anyone. The dog counts.
