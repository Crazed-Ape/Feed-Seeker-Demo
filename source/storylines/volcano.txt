▫️ **The bottle**

A lemonade bottle is perfect. Rinse it out, peel off the label, and put the lid away in a drawer so nobody screws it back on by accident.

▫️ **Building the mountain**

Do these in order. Each step has a check, and you only move on when the check is true.

1. Stand the bottle in the middle of the tray. Check: it doesn't wobble.
2. Roll half the clay into fat snakes, about as thick as your thumb. Check: half the clay is still in the bag.
3. Wrap the snakes around the bottle from the bottom up, wider at the bottom. Check: the mountain is about twice as wide at the bottom as at the top.
4. Stop the clay just below the opening of the bottle. Check: you can see the whole opening, with no clay inside it.
5. Smooth the clay with wet fingers and add some rocky bumps. Check: you can't see the bottle through any gaps.
6. Add the rest of the clay at the bottom if it looks thin. Check: the bottle is hidden everywhere except the opening.

▫️ **The cave**

Yes! Push a pencil into the side to make a little cave, but only into the clay, never through to the bottle. If the bottle gets a hole, the lava comes out of the cave instead of the top. That would be an interesting bug, but not the one we want.

=== m volcano.18 thread=volcano-main side=u from=p3 to=c7
Why does it fizz? Is it the same as fizzy drinks? Would it fizz in my tummy if I ate baking soda? (I won't.)

From {{p3.name}} and {{pick.dog}}

=== m volcano.19 thread=volcano-main side=a from=c7 to=p3 turn=t8 part=1
{{c7.emoji}} **{{c7.name}}** · {{date}} · {{time}}

▫️ **Why it fizzes**

Vinegar has an acid in it, called acetic acid. Baking soda is the opposite kind of thing, a base, called sodium bicarbonate. When they meet, they swap pieces and make three new things: water, a kind of salt, and carbon dioxide gas. The gas makes the bubbles. The dish soap catches the bubbles and turns them into foam, so they pile up and pour out of the top instead of popping straight away.

▫️ **Fizzy drinks**

Yes! It's the same gas, carbon dioxide. In a drink, the gas is squeezed in before the bottle is closed. In your volcano, the gas is made right there in the bottle.

▫️ **Your tummy**

A little baking soda goes into cakes and pancakes, and that's fine. Eating lots of it on its own can make you feel sick, so the lava stays a not-for-eating thing. You were right to say you won't.

That's a great question for the poster, by the way: "What makes the lava move?"

▫️ **Two bugs, one fizzle**

Both of those cut the gas, and they add up:

1. A teaspoon holds a third of a tablespoon, so only a third of the baking soda went in.
2. Vinegar topped up with water has half the acid, so it gives about half the push.

The vinegar matters most. With the right spoon there's more baking soda than the vinegar can use, so the amount of gas depends on how much acid goes in. Half-strength vinegar means about half the gas. The small spoonful made the fizz start slowly, so the foam never built into a rush. A blub is exactly what those amounts should make. The volcano works; the recipe just didn't get a fair chance.

▫️ **The fix**

1. 2 tablespoons of baking soda, measured with the big spoon.
2. Half a cup of vinegar from a new bottle, with no water added.
3. Pour it all in at once, fast.
