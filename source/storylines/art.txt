=== m art.08 thread=art-main side=a from=c4 to=p2 turn=t3 part=1 phase=backstory at=-2d10:02
{{c4.emoji}} **{{c4.name}}** · {{date}} · {{time}}

▫️ **Print cost**

They cost the same. Both print in standard four-color on the same card stock, and the press doesn't care how many colors are inside the picture. The woodcut could be printed as two special inks instead, but that only saves money on runs far bigger than ours. At our quantity it would cost more, so I'd print both the standard way.

▫️ **Who paints it**

Nobody needs a brush. I'd build the watercolor digitally from scanned paper and real wash textures, and it's one back for the whole deck, so either way it's one picture, not a hundred. The real difference is time: about a day per revision for the woodcut, and about two for the watercolor.

▫️ **The distance test**

I printed all four tiles and checked them at 70 cm, as {{p1.name}} asked:

1. Woodcut, lantern alone: clear.
2. Woodcut, lantern on leaves: clear, and the leaves read as texture without competing.
3. Watercolor, lantern alone: clear, a bit soft.
4. Watercolor, lantern on leaves: the lantern dissolves into the leaves past about 60 cm. A thin outline on the lantern fixes it.\
↳ `Y1`, `Y2`

Nine fit on an A4 page, in three rows of three, with crop marks. I've added a 50 mm test bar along the bottom edge, because the most common way to ruin a print sheet is a printer quietly shrinking it to fit the page.

▫️ **Steps, in this order**

1. Open the sheet and print it at **actual size** (100%), not "fit to page".
   1. Check: the test bar measures 50 mm with a ruler. If it doesn't, stop and fix the print setting before going on.
2. Print as many copies as you need, one for every nine cards, on the heaviest paper your printer takes.
   1. Check: rub a corner after a minute; nothing smudges.
3. Cut along the crop marks, outer edges first.
   1. Check: one cut card measures 63 × 88 mm.
4. Glue or sleeve each back onto a prototype card.
   1. Check: the lantern sits in the middle, within about 1 mm.\
↳ `T5`

3. Cut along the crop marks, outer edges first.
   1. Check: one cut card measures 63 × 88 mm.
4. Glue or sleeve each back onto a prototype card.
