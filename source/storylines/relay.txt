=== m relay.09 thread=relay-research side=a from=c2 to=p1 turn=t1 part=2 files=ra2
{{c2.emoji}} **{{c2.name}}** · {{date}} · {{time}}

▫️ **What similar games do**

I measured the resource icons on twelve cozy cooperative games, the ones on the studio's shelf and the ones at {{pick.cafe}}. I worked from photos with a ruler in frame.

1. 9 of the 12 give each resource its own shape as well as its own color.
2. The other 3 use color alone, and all 3 print a symbol key on every card to make up for it.
3. Icons on the card faces run from 8 to 11 mm. Ours are 7 mm.
4. Icons in cost rows, where they sit small, run from 5 to 7 mm. Ours are 5 mm.

▫️ **Why it matters**

The commonly cited figure is that about 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some red-green color vision deficiency. At a table of five, the chance that someone sees our colors differently is real. Your tester may also have been fighting dim café light, though. Shell and wool are both pale, so this could be a lighting problem as much as a color one.

▫️ **How sure I am**

The sizes come from photos, so trust them to about a millimeter. Twelve games is a shelf, not a market survey. The color-vision figure is the general one, not a count of board gamers.

▫️ **Next step**

The icons are {{c4.name}}'s work, so I've written a prompt asking whether ours survive grayscale and the common color-blind simulations, and what size they are now. It follows this message.\

I ran all six icons through grayscale and both red-green simulations at real print size, side by side. The sheet is attached so you can see what I saw.

2. **Kelp and lantern, in both red-green simulations.** Sea green and brick red turn into the same olive brown, and both icons are tall and narrow.

▫️ **What passes**

1. **Bread and honey** stay distinct in every view. Honey is amber, by the way, not orange. I checked twice, for everyone's sake.

1. Face icons go from 7 to 9 mm, cost-row icons from 5 to 6 mm.
2. Each resource gets a silhouette that works with no color at all: a spiral shell, wavy kelp, a square lantern, a round bun, cloud-shaped wool and a drop of honey.
3. A darker outline on every icon.
4. A second cue in one corner of each card, so the resource still reads when a thumb covers the icon.
