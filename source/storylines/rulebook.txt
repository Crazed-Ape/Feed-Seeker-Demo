=== m rulebook.05 thread=rulebook-main side=a from=c3 to=p2 turn=t2 part=2 card=doc
{{c3.emoji}} **{{c3.name}}** · {{date}} · {{time}}

▫️ **Why the number matters**

The Tide deck has 54 cards. At the end of every round the group reveals five, and the game ends when a full five can't be revealed. So every card dealt into a hand at setup is a card the clock doesn't get. {{p1.name}}'s design notes aim for eight rounds, which is what fits in forty minutes.

▫️ **The arithmetic**

| Players | 4 cards each | 3 cards each |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 46 left, 9 rounds | 48 left, 9 rounds |
| 3 | 42 left, 8 rounds | 45 left, 9 rounds |
| 4 | 38 left, 7 rounds | 42 left, 8 rounds |
| 5 | 34 left, 6 rounds | 39 left, 7 rounds |

Four cards for everyone gives a five-player game only six rounds, and that's too few to finish the festival tasks. Four cards for two or three players and three cards for four or five keeps every player count within a round of the target.\
↳ `Y1`

---

❓ **Choices**

> 💠 `Y1` Starting hand size:\
> ⭐ **A** Four cards with two or three players, three with four or five\
> ➖ **B** Four cards for everyone, simpler to explain, shorter big games

Honestly: not everyone. But the people who do read it matter a lot.

▫️ **Who reads the example**

1. **The person teaching the game.** One player at each table reads the whole book before the first game, and the example is how they check they've understood a turn before explaining it to four people.

The card can hold the order of a turn. It can't hold the reasons, like why you'd keep a Tide card instead of playing it. The example is the only place the book shows a player thinking.
