▫️ **Lesson 1: hello, please, thank you**

1. **Guten Morgen** (GOO-ten MOR-gen): good morning. Use it until about eleven.
2. **Guten Tag**: hello. This is the safe greeting for the rest of the day.
3. **Hallo**: hi. Friendly, and fine with other exhibitors.
4. **Danke** / **Danke schön**: thanks / thank you very much.
5. **Bitte**: please. It also means "you're welcome", which is handy.
6. **Entschuldigung**: excuse me, or sorry.
7. **Auf Wiedersehen** / **Tschüss**: goodbye, formal and casual.

▫️ **Sie and du**

German has two words for "you". **Sie** is polite, for strangers and anyone selling you something. **du** is for friends. From what I've read, people at game fairs move to **du** quickly. I'd still start with **Sie** and follow the other person's lead. Nobody minds being treated politely.

▫️ **Today's practice**

Say three of these out loud, anywhere, to anyone, including the dog.\

Yes, they say the whole thing, and yes, it's that long. It's easier in four beats: **ent-SHOOL-di-gung**, with the weight on the second one. People sometimes shorten it to **'tschuldigung** in casual speech. I'd use the full word, though, since it's never wrong.

1. **Möchten Sie es ausprobieren?** Would you like to try it?
2. **Das ist ein kooperatives Spiel.** It's a cooperative game.
3. **Alle spielen zusammen.** Everyone plays together.
4. **Für zwei bis fünf Spieler.** For two to five players.
5. **Eine Partie dauert ungefähr vierzig Minuten.** A game takes about forty minutes.

▫️ **Numbers you just used**

1. **eins, zwei, drei, vier, fünf**: one to five.
2. **vierzig**: forty. It's **vier** (four) plus **-zig**, and most tens are built the same way.

Together, lines 1 to 5 are a whole pitch. Try it twice, start to finish.\

=== m language.13 thread=language-main side=a from=c2 to=p1 turn=t6 part=1
{{c2.emoji}} **{{c2.name}}** · {{date}} · {{time}}

Very normal. Long words come back with use, not with staring at them. **aus-pro-BEE-ren**, "try out". Today's lesson is the one that rescues you when words don't come back.

▫️ **Lesson 3: when you don't understand**

1. **Wie bitte?** Sorry? / Pardon? The quickest one.
2. **Können Sie das bitte wiederholen?** Could you repeat that, please?
3. **Langsamer, bitte.** Slower, please.
4. **Ich verstehe nicht.** I don't understand.
5. **Ich spreche nur ein bisschen Deutsch.** I only speak a little German.
6. **Sprechen Sie Englisch?** Do you speak English?

▫️ **Why this one matters**

In my reading on phrasebook learners, the common trouble isn't saying things. It's the reply, which comes back fast and full. Line 5 gets most people to slow down kindly. Line 3 does the rest.

Line 2 is a mouthful, so it's today's practice.\

▫️ **Lesson 4: numbers and prices**

1. **zehn, zwanzig, dreißig, vierzig, fünfzig**: ten, twenty, thirty, forty, fifty.
2. **hundert**: a hundred.
3. **Was kostet das?** What does it cost?
4. **Das kostet fünfundzwanzig.** It costs twenty-five.
5. **Ab welcher Stückzahl gibt es einen Rabatt?** From what quantity is there a discount?
6. **Haben Sie eine Visitenkarte?** Do you have a business card?

▫️ **The tricky bit**

German says the ones before the tens: 25 is **fünfundzwanzig**, "five-and-twenty". 42 is **zweiundvierzig**, "two-and-forty". When someone says a price, the last part you hear is the tens, so wait for the end before you decide. I'd write the number down rather than trust your ear on the first day. Fair stands usually have a pen.

▫️ **Practice**

Read five prices off any receipt aloud, in German.\

▫️ **Review 1: Sie and du**

With **Sie**, the verb ends in **-en**: **Möchten Sie**, **Kommen Sie**. With **du**, it ends in **-st**: **Möchtest du**, **Kommst du**. Your 3 and 9 mixed them. For polite, look for **-en**.

▫️ **Review 2: numbers backwards**

**zweiundfünfzig** is 52, so you said the numbers the English way round. In German the ones come first, then **und**, then the tens:

1. 25: **fünf-und-zwanzig**.

▫️ **Review 3: the right rescue phrase**

**Langsamer, bitte** asks for slower, which is useful but answers a different question. For "Pardon?", the short one is **Wie bitte?**
