# Card Readability Notes: Resource Icons

Research for {{pick.studio}}, prompted by the "shell or wool??" note on a playtest score sheet.

## Method

1. Twelve cozy cooperative games: six from the studio shelf, six from the shelves at {{pick.cafe}}.
2. Each resource card was photographed flat with a ruler in frame. Icons were measured from the photos.
3. "Face" means the main resource icon on the card. "Cost row" means the small icons in a card's cost or requirement line.
4. Games are listed by shelf position, not by title. The titles don't matter here, only the icons.

## What the table says

1. 9 of the 12 give each resource its own shape as well as its own color.
2. The other 3 use color alone, and all 3 print a symbol key on every card to make up for it.
3. Face icons run from 8 to 11 mm. Ours are 7 mm, below the smallest on the shelf.
4. Cost-row icons run from 5 to 7 mm. Ours are 5 mm, at the bottom of the range.
5. No game in the twelve relies on color alone without some extra help on the card.

## Color vision

The commonly cited figure is that about 1 in 12 men and 1 in 200 women have some red-green color vision deficiency. That is the general figure, not a count of board gamers. At a table of five it is a real chance, not a rare one.

Lighting matters too. Shell and wool are both pale, and the playtest was in café light, so the tester's trouble may be partly a lighting problem as much as a color one.

## How sure I am

1. Sizes come from photos, so trust them to about a millimeter.
2. Twelve games is a shelf, not a market survey.
3. Whether our own icons survive grayscale and color-blind simulations is a question for {{c4.name}}, not something these photos can answer.
