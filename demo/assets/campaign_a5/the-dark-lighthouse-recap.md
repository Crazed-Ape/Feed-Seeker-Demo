# The Dark Lighthouse: A Recap

## Saltmarrow, at dusk

For three nights the lighthouse on Gullstone Point had stood dark over the gray bay, and nobody in Saltmarrow liked it. The old Keeper had climbed up to fix the lamp and never come down. The *Merry Halibut* was due home at midnight. A storm was piling up on the horizon like a heap of dirty laundry, and the town looked around and decided the job was ours.

There were four hours on the storm clock when three unlikely heroes stood outside the Drowned Lantern: the Cartographer, the Knight of Modest Means and the Herbalist.

## The Drowned Lantern

We started with soup, which is how all good adventures should start. The innkeeper, flour to her elbows, studied the Knight's buttons, decided they were *good* buttons, and gave us the soup for free once she worked out where we were headed. She told us what we needed: the lamp burns with an Ember Stone that wants waking now and then, the cliff road crosses the river Gall, and a troll keeps the gate in the pass.

The Cartographer's compass swung once and pointed firmly at the pie case. The Herbalist bought a warm bun for one button. Barley the horse stayed in the stable with his oats, having earned the night off.

## The road north

The cliff road ran along the edge with the sea grumbling below. The river Gall had its own opinions about being crossed, and we got over it anyway, a little shaken and well into the storm clock.

At the narrow pass, a troll the size of a garden shed sat knitting seaweed on a stool far too small for it. It opens the gate for an answer or for cheese, and it is a little fussy about the cheese. One way or another, the gate swung open, and the troll went back to its knitting looking pleased.

## Two hundred and twelve steps

The rain came all at once, like thrown gravel, and we ran the last stretch of the Point. Inside the lighthouse a spiral stair climbed into the dark, two hundred and twelve steps with a long drop down the middle.

The Cartographer, who is famously not scared of heights, rolled a 6. The Knight held their hand the whole way up, which was worth exactly the 2 they needed. They counted every step out loud and never once looked down the middle.

At the top we found the Keeper, wrapped in a blanket with a twisted ankle and a bad temper, very much alive. The wind had blown the door in and knocked the Keeper flat, and the lamp had gone out with them. Far out on the black water, a tiny light bobbed and dipped. The *Merry Halibut* was out there.

## The bell

The Keeper had seen this kind of storm before: it always turns at the Point. Usually. So we trusted the Keeper. The Knight's player said that was the brave choice, and the Knight's player was right, because waiting is the hardest kind of brave.

The Knight hauled on the bell rope, and the great bell boomed out over the water, slow and steady, once a minute. The tower shook. The rain hammered the glass. The little light of the *Merry Halibut* bobbed, disappeared, and bobbed again. "Long hour, this," said the Keeper. "Always is."

## The duel with the hedge

Between the bells, we told stories, and the Knight told the best one.

It was, she explained, a very rude hedge. It blocked a lane. It scratched a horse. It would not apologize. The duel lasted from noon until supper. The Knight won on points, the hedge won on stamina, and they parted with mutual respect and a great many leaves in the Knight's hair. The Keeper laughed so hard they forgot their ankle. The Herbalist said he has spoken to hedges, and they tell the story differently.

## The storm turns

Somewhere near the last bell before dawn, the wind changed its mind. It bent around the Point exactly as the Keeper promised, and the rain softened to a drizzle, then to nothing.

At first light the *Merry Halibut* came into harbor, a little battered and entirely whole. "We followed the bell," the skipper told the town. "Slow and steady, once a minute."

## At dawn

1. The Knight of Modest Means was famous by breakfast, for the hedge.
2. The Herbalist splinted the Keeper's ankle and declared it "sprained but optimistic".
3. The Cartographer drew a small bell in the last blank space on the map, with sound lines coming off it.

The lamp is still dark, and the Ember Stone still glows its sleepy red. "We'll wake it properly," said the Keeper, "next time you're passing. Bring the hedge story."

*The end, for now. The lamp is waiting.*
