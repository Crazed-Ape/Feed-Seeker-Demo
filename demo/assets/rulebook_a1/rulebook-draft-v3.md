# {{pick.game}} · Rulebook

*Third draft · 16 pages*

## Page 1 · Welcome

Welcome, dear neighbor! Pour yourself something warm, gather your favorite people, and let the sea breeze carry you into a cozy day of festival preparations.

### Goal of the game

The harbor festival begins at sunset, and nothing is ready. Work together to finish all six festival tasks before the Tide deck runs out. Everyone wins together, or everyone loses together.

For 2 to 5 players, about 40 minutes.

## Page 2 · The harbor

The board shows the harbor and its six docks, joined by a path that runs all the way around the water:

1. The Pier
2. The Fish Market
3. Bakery Row
4. The Lighthouse
5. The Boathouse
6. The Ferry Slip

The path is a loop: after the Ferry Slip comes the Pier again. Each dock has one task space, where a festival task card sits during the game. Neighbors walk from dock to dock, do the work each task needs, and keep an eye on the water.

## Page 3 · Setup

1. Unfold the harbor board in the middle of the table.
2. Shuffle the 10 festival task cards. Place one face up on the task space of each dock, and return the other four to the box without looking at them.
3. Each player chooses a neighbor card and takes the matching pawn. Place every pawn on the Pier. Put the shells in a pile beside the board: this is the shell supply.
4. Shuffle the Tide deck and deal some cards to each player.
5. Reveal five Tide cards face up in a row beside the board. This is the tide line. Ignore any waves on these first five cards: no task has shells on it yet, so there's nothing to wash away.
6. The player who most recently stood on a beach goes first.

Keep your Tide cards in your hand. You may show them to the others whenever you like.

## Page 5 · The cards

### Festival task cards

Each task card shows a name and five spaces, and each space shows a symbol: net, lantern, bread, flower or rope. A task is finished when all five of its spaces are covered.

### Tide cards

The Tide deck has 54 cards. Each card shows one symbol. Some also show a wave and a picture of a dock: those are the cards that wash work away at the end of a round (see page 8). One card in the deck is the ferry card.

### Neighbor cards

Each neighbor has one ability:

1. **The Herbalist:** once per turn, you may play a flower card as any symbol.
2. **The Baker:** when you take a card from the tide line, take two, then put one back.
3. **The Lamplighter:** you may move up to three docks instead of two.
4. **The Net Mender:** waves never wash shells off the task at your dock.
5. **The Harbor Master:** you may trade with a neighbor at any dock, not only your own.

### Card diagram

*Diagram: a festival task card and a Tide card, side by side, with the icon legend from the card layouts.*

1. **Symbol:** the kind of work this card does.
2. **Wave:** at the end of a round, this card washes one token off the task at the dock shown.
3. **Dock picture:** where the wave lands.
4. **Task space:** place a token here when you play a matching card.

## Page 6 · A turn

Play goes clockwise. On your turn, do these four steps in order:

1. Move
2. Take or trade
3. Work
4. Rest

You may talk about your plans at any time, including on other players' turns. Talking is not a step. It's the whole game.

### 1. Move

Move your pawn up to two docks along the path, in either direction. You may also stay where you are. Any number of pawns can share a dock.

### 2. Take or trade

Choose one:

1. **Take:** take one card from the tide line into your hand. Don't refill the tide line.
2. **Trade:** swap one card with a neighbor at your dock. You both have to agree, and you can each look at what's on offer first.

## Page 7 · A turn, continued

### 3. Work

Play any number of Tide cards whose symbols match empty spaces on the task at your dock. For each card you play, take one token from the supply and place it on the matching space. Played cards go to a discard pile beside the deck.

When the fifth token lands on a task, the task is finished. Turn the card sideways so everyone can see it's done. Its tokens stay where they are, and the tide can't touch them.

### 4. Rest

If you have more than five cards in your hand, discard down to five. Then the player on your left takes a turn.

### The ferry card

Instead of moving in step 1, you may play the ferry card to move your pawn to any dock. Then discard it.

## Page 8 · The Tide

The Tide deck is the clock. Nobody draws from it during a turn. It only shrinks at the end of each round, when the tide comes in.

A round ends after every player has taken one turn. Then:

1. Discard any cards left in the tide line.
2. Reveal five Tide cards from the deck to make a new tide line.
3. For each new card with a wave, find the dock it shows. If the task at that dock is unfinished and has at least one token on it, remove one token and return it to the supply.
4. The player on the left of this round's first player starts the next round.

## Page 9 · The Tide, continued

### Reading the water

1. Count the deck often. Five cards leave it every round, and when it can't give you five more, the day is over.
2. A task with four shells and a wave on the way is a task to finish this turn.
3. Finished tasks are safe. The tide only takes from work that isn't done.
4. The tide line is shared. Before you take a card, ask whether someone else needs it more.

### Waves and the Net Mender

If a wave lands on the dock where the Net Mender's pawn stands, nothing washes away. Plan the Net Mender's walk around the tasks you can't finish yet.

## Page 10 · The end of the game

The game ends at the end of the round in which you can't reveal five Tide cards. If all players agree, you may win. If any festival task is still unfinished, the harbor isn't ready, and you all lose together.

Then turn the page and see how close you came.

## Page 11 · Scoring

Win or lose, it's worth knowing how close you came. Count the markers on the board. Every finished task holds five markers, so a harbor with all six tasks done scores 30. Markers the tide washed away don't count.

| Score | The festival |
|---|---|
| 30 | The lanterns are lit and the music starts on time. |
| 25 to 29 | The festival opens late, but it opens. |
| 20 to 24 | Half the town ends up helping. Try again tomorrow. |
| Under 20 | The tide had other plans. |

## Page 12 · Designer's note

This game started on a gray morning by the water, watching people string up lights for a festival while the tide crept up the slipway. Nobody was in charge, and everything got done anyway, mostly because everyone kept talking to each other.

I wanted a game that felt like that morning. There's no winner among you, no hidden score and no reason to keep secrets. The only opponent is the tide, and it doesn't care how clever you are, only how well you plan together.

If you lose, you'll know exactly why, and you'll probably want to try again straight away. That's the game working.

## Pages 13–14 · Example of play

This example follows one whole round of a three-player game, from the first turn to the moment the tide comes in. It's the start of round 3, and the neighbors take their turns in this order: the Baker, the Net Mender and the Herbalist.

*Diagram: the table at the start of round 3.*

### Where things stand

1. Bake for the Stalls, at Bakery Row, is finished. It has five shells on it and is turned sideways.
2. Mend the Nets, at the Fish Market, has two shells on it. It still needs a net and two ropes.
3. Hang the Lanterns, at the Lighthouse, has two shells on it. It had three, but a wave washed one away at the end of round 2. It still needs two lanterns and a rope.
4. Paint the Boats, at the Boathouse, has one token on it.
5. The other two tasks haven't been started.
6. The Tide deck has 27 cards left. The tide line shows a net, a bread, a flower, a second bread and a second flower.

### The hands

1. The Baker is at the Pier with a net, a rope and a bread.
2. The Net Mender is at the Fish Market with a lantern, two ropes and a net.
3. The Herbalist is at the Lighthouse with a lantern, two flowers and a bread.

### The plan

Before anyone moves, the table talks it over. Remember that you can talk about your plans at any time, and you can show each other your cards whenever you like. Talking is not a step of the turn, so it never uses up anything.

The Baker looks at the Fish Market, one dock away, and at the tide line, which has no rope in it. "I'll take the nets if you can cover the lanterns," the Baker says to the Net Mender. "But I need one of your ropes."

The Net Mender agrees. That still leaves the Net Mender with a lantern and a rope, which is most of what the Lighthouse needs, and the Lighthouse is two docks from the Fish Market.

### The Baker's turn

1. **Move.** The Baker moves one dock, from the Pier to the Fish Market. Remember that you can move up to two docks, in either direction, or stay where you are.
2. **Take or trade.** The Baker chooses to trade rather than take. Trading means swapping one card with a neighbor at your dock, and the Net Mender is at the Fish Market. The Baker gives the bread and gets a rope. Both players agreed, so the trade happens.
3. **Work.** The Baker plays the net and both ropes. Each card matches an empty space on Mend the Nets, so the Baker takes three shells from the supply and puts one on each matching space. That makes five, so the task is finished, and the Baker turns it sideways.
4. **Rest.** The Baker has no cards left. An empty hand isn't a problem, and there's no hand limit to check. The tide line refills at the end of the round.

### The Net Mender's turn

1. **Move.** The Net Mender moves two docks, from the Fish Market past Bakery Row to the Lighthouse. Two docks is the most you can move in a turn, unless you're the Lamplighter.
2. **Take or trade.** The Net Mender takes one bread from the tide line. Nothing needs it today, but Set the Tables, at the Ferry Slip, will need three of them later. Taking a card doesn't refill the tide line, so four cards are left in it.
3. **Work.** The Net Mender plays the lantern and the rope on Hang the Lanterns and places two tokens. The task now has four.
4. **Rest.** The Net Mender has three cards: the bread from the trade, the bread from the tide line and the net. That's under five, so there's nothing to discard.

### The Herbalist's turn

The Herbalist is already at the Lighthouse, and Hang the Lanterns needs one more lantern. There are two ways to finish it: play the lantern, or play a flower as a lantern using the Herbalist's ability. Either way, the task is finished.

The Herbalist plays the real lantern and keeps both flowers. A lantern is only ever a lantern, but a flower in the Herbalist's hand can be anything next turn, and the harbor still has three tasks to go.

1. **Move.** The Herbalist stays at the Lighthouse. Staying where you are is allowed.
2. **Take or trade.** The Herbalist takes a flower from the tide line, for the same reason as before: flowers can become anything in the Herbalist's hand.
3. **Work.** The Herbalist plays the lantern and places the fifth shell. Hang the Lanterns is finished and turned sideways.
4. **Rest.** The Herbalist has four cards: three flowers and a bread. That's under five, so there's nothing to discard.

### The end of the round

Every player has had a turn, so the round ends.

1. The cards left in the tide line, a net, a bread and a flower, go to the discard pile.
2. The group reveals five new Tide cards: a rope, a flower, a lantern with a wave showing the Lighthouse, a net, and a bread with a wave showing the Boathouse.
3. The wave at the Lighthouse does nothing, because Hang the Lanterns is finished and the tide can't touch finished tasks. The wave at the Boathouse takes the only shell off Paint the Boats, and it goes back to the supply.
4. The Net Mender, on the left of the Baker, starts round 4.

If the Net Mender had stayed at the Boathouse, that shell would have survived, but then the lanterns would still be waiting. Nobody can be in two places at once. The deck now has 22 cards, three tasks are finished, and the whole table already knows what the Herbalist is saving those flowers for.
