# Poster Text: What Makes the Lava Move?

A science-fair project by {{p3.name}}

Four boxes, one idea each. Keep the letters big enough to read from a step away.

## Box 1: The question

What makes the lava move?

My volcano erupts red foam instead of real lava. Real lava is about a thousand degrees, which is far too hot for a school fair. I wanted to find out what pushes the foam up and out of the top.

## Box 2: What we did

1. We built a mountain of gray modeling clay around a half-liter plastic bottle, with a small cave in one side.
2. We put 2 tablespoons of baking soda, a squirt of dish soap and a few drops of red food coloring into the bottle.
3. We poured in half a cup of vinegar all at once, and stepped back.
4. We counted how many seconds the foam kept coming and how high it went.
5. We always wore goggles, and a grown-up did the pouring.

## Box 3: What happens

Vinegar has an acid in it, called acetic acid.
Baking soda is a base, called sodium bicarbonate.

When an acid and a base meet, they make three new things: water, a kind of salt, and carbon dioxide gas.

The gas makes bubbles. The dish soap catches the bubbles and turns them into foam. The foam has nowhere to go but up, so it pours out of the top like lava.

Carbon dioxide is the same gas that makes fizzy drinks fizzy.
