# Volcano Shopping List

Enough for one test run, one spare go and the science fair. Everything except the goggles should be in one trip to the grocery store.

## What to buy

| # | Item | Amount | Notes |
|---|------|--------|-------|
| 1 | White vinegar | 2 bottles, about 1 liter each | Plain white vinegar. Keep one bottle unopened for the fair. |
| 2 | Baking soda | 1 box, about 500 grams | Plenty left over for pancakes. |
| 3 | Dish soap | A few squirts | The bottle by the sink is fine. |
| 4 | Food coloring | 1 small bottle | Red only, as requested. Not orange. |
| 5 | Non-drying modeling clay | About 1 kilo | Brown or gray. It never sets, so it can be reshaped. |
| 6 | Plastic bottle | 1, half a liter, narrow neck | An empty drinks bottle, rinsed, label off. |
| 7 | Tray, bucket, towels | 1 tray with a lip, 1 bucket, 2 old towels | The tray catches the lava. The bucket takes what's left. |
| 8 | Goggles | 2 pairs | Swimming goggles count. One for the pourer, one for the scientist. |

## What one eruption uses

| Ingredient | Amount per go |
|------------|---------------|
| Baking soda | 2 tbsp |
| Dish soap | 1 squirt |
| Red coloring | A few drops |
| White vinegar | Half a cup, poured all at once |

## Why this is enough

1. Half a cup of vinegar is a little over 100 milliliters, so one liter covers the test run, the spare go and several eruptions at the fair, with the second bottle as backup.
2. Two tablespoons of baking soda is about 30 grams, so one box covers far more eruptions than anyone will want to clean up after.
3. A kilo of clay is enough to build a mountain about twice as wide at the bottom as at the top, with some left over to patch thin spots.

## Already at home (check before buying)

1. Tablespoon and measuring jug with a half-cup line.
2. A sheet of paper to roll into a funnel.
3. A drawer to put the bottle's lid in, so nobody screws it back on.

## Not on the list on purpose

1. Anything hot. The lava is foam, not fire.
2. A lid. The gas always needs a way out.
