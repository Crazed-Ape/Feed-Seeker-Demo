# German for the Fair: Vocabulary

Version 2 · Lessons 1 to 4

## How to read the hints

The hints are sound-alikes, not exact spellings. Capital letters show where the weight goes.

## Lesson 1: hello, please, thank you

| # | German | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guten Morgen | GOO-ten MOR-gen | Good morning (until about eleven) |
| 2 | Guten Tag | GOO-ten TAHK | Hello |
| 3 | Hallo | HAH-lo | Hi |
| 4 | Danke / Danke schön | DAHN-kuh / DAHN-kuh shern | Thanks / Thank you very much |
| 5 | Bitte | BIT-tuh | Please; you're welcome |
| 6 | Entschuldigung | ent-SHOOL-di-gung | Excuse me; sorry |
| 7 | Auf Wiedersehen / Tschüss | owf VEE-der-zay-en / choos | Goodbye, formal / casual |

In casual speech you'll sometimes hear **'tschuldigung**. The full word is never wrong, so use that.

## Lesson 2: at the booth

| # | German | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Möchten Sie es ausprobieren? | MERKH-ten zee es ows-pro-BEE-ren | Would you like to try it? |
| 2 | Das ist ein kooperatives Spiel. | dahs ist ine ko-op-er-ah-TEE-ves shpeel | It's a cooperative game. |
| 3 | Alle spielen zusammen. | AH-luh SHPEE-len tsoo-ZAH-men | Everyone plays together. |
| 4 | Für zwei bis fünf Spieler. | fuer tsvy bis fuenf SHPEE-ler | For two to five players. |
| 5 | Eine Partie dauert ungefähr vierzig Minuten. | EYE-nuh par-TEE DOW-ert OON-guh-fair FEER-tsikh mi-NOO-ten | A game takes about forty minutes. |

Lines 1 to 5 together are the whole pitch.

**ausprobieren** means "try out". It's aus-pro-BEE-ren, and it comes back with use.

## Lesson 3: when you don't understand

| # | German | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wie bitte? | vee BIT-tuh | Sorry? / Pardon? |
| 2 | Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? | KER-nen zee dahs BIT-tuh vee-der-HOH-len | Could you repeat that, please? |
| 3 | Langsamer, bitte. | LAHNG-zah-mer BIT-tuh | Slower, please. |
| 4 | Ich verstehe nicht. | ikh fer-SHTAY-uh nikht | I don't understand. |
| 5 | Ich spreche nur ein bisschen Deutsch. | ikh SHPREKH-uh noor ine BISS-khen doytch | I only speak a little German. |
| 6 | Sprechen Sie Englisch? | SHPREKH-en zee ENG-lish | Do you speak English? |

Line 5 gets most people to slow down kindly. Line 3 does the rest.

## Lesson 4: numbers and prices

### Numbers

| Number | German | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | eins | ines |
| 2 | zwei | tsvy |
| 3 | drei | dry |
| 4 | vier | feer |
| 5 | fünf | fuenf |
| 10 | zehn | tsayn |
| 20 | zwanzig | TSVAN-tsikh |
| 30 | dreißig | DRY-sikh |
| 40 | vierzig | FEER-tsikh |
| 50 | fünfzig | FUENF-tsikh |
| 100 | hundert | HOON-dert |

Most tens are a number plus **-zig**: **vier** plus **-zig** is **vierzig**. Thirty is the odd one out, with **-ßig**.

### Prices

| # | German | Sounds like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Was kostet das? | vahs KOSS-tet dahs | What does it cost? |
| 2 | Das kostet fünfundzwanzig. | dahs KOSS-tet FUENF-oont-tsvan-tsikh | It costs twenty-five. |
| 3 | Ab welcher Stückzahl gibt es einen Rabatt? | ahp VEL-kher SHTUECK-tsahl gipt es EYE-nen rah-BAHT | From what quantity is there a discount? |
| 4 | Haben Sie eine Visitenkarte? | HAH-ben zee EYE-nuh vi-ZEE-ten-kar-tuh | Do you have a business card? |

### The tricky bit

German says the ones before the tens.

1. 25 is **fünfundzwanzig**: "five-and-twenty".
2. 42 is **zweiundvierzig**: "two-and-forty".

The last thing you hear is the tens, so wait for the end before you decide. On the first day, write the number down rather than trust your ear.

## Sie and du

Start with **Sie** (polite) and follow the other person's lead. At game fairs, people often move to **du** quickly.
